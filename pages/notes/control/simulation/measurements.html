Generation of measurements

Topics

Consider at time step $k$, the following position measurement $y$ is generated from the actual position $x_q$. $$ y_k = C \; x_k + \sqrt V \; \eta $$
$$\begin{array}{ccl} y &:& \text{Measurement} \\ \eta &:& \text{Random variable drawn from a standard normal distribution } N(0, 1) \\ \sqrt V &:& \text{Standard deviation scaling of the noise term.} \end{array}$$
Variance is a single, deterministic number that describes the average spread of the distribution — it does not contain the random variable $\eta$ itself. Consider individual simulated samples, the squared deviation of a specific measurement's noise from the mean would be $(\sqrt{V}\eta)^2 = V\eta^2$. But the average of the deviations across the entire distribution, the expected value of $\eta^2$ is $1$. Thus the variance is just $V$.

Without $\eta$, $\sqrt{V}$ is just a constant offset. Every single time you simulate or compute this measurement, it would shift the exact same amount. It wouldn't be "noise" anymore—it would just be a known bias.

By multiplying $\sqrt{V}$ by $\eta$, you are telling the simulation:
  • Pick a random direction and intensity: That's $\eta$. It samples from a standard normal distribution, giving you a random number (sometimes positive, sometimes negative, usually between $-2$ and $2$).

  • Scale it to the right size: Multiplying that random sample by $\sqrt{V}$ stretches or shrinks that standard normal bell curve so that it matches the actual variance $V$ of your physical sensor.
