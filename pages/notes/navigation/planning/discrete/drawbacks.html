Drawbacks

Performing a complete discretisation of a large space such as those found in higher dimensional problems and applying a graph search algorithm to the space may be too costly. The complexity of the path planning problem increases exponentially with the number of dimensions in the C-space.

Increased Dimensionality

For a 2-dimensional 8-connected space, every node has 8 successors (it is possible to move from every cell laterally or diagonally). A 3-dimensional 8-connected space has 26 successors from every node. As the dimension of the C-space grows, the number of successors that every cell has increases substantially. For an n-dimensional space, it is equal to $3^n - 1$.

If multiple 6-DOF arms work in a common space, the computation required to perform path planning to avoid collisions increases substantially. Planning for humanoid robots may take intolerably long to solve using the combinatorial approach.

Constrained Dynamics

For instance, a car is limited in its motion - it can move forward and backward, and it can turn with a limited turning radius. However, the car is not able to move laterally

In the case of the car, more complex motion dynamics must be considered when path planning - including the derivatives of the state variables such as velocity. For example, a car's safe turning radius is dependent on it's velocity.

Robotic systems can be classified into two different categories:
  • Holonomic systems
    • They can be defined as systems where every constraint depends exclusively on the current pose and time, and not on any derivatives with respect to time.

  • Nonholonomic systems
    • They are dependent on derivatives. Path planning for nonholonomic systems is more difficult due to the added constraints.
