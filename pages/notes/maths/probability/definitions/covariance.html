Covariance

Use

A covariance matrix summarises the pairwise correlations between variables in a dataset. It quantifies the spread, uncertainty, or variability of a dataset.

Features

A covariance matrix is
  • A square matrix.
  • Symmetric when the covariance between variables $i$ and $j$ (2D example) is the same as that between variables $j$ and $i$.

Linear transformation

The covariance matrix of a random vector $x$ is defined as the expected value of the outer product of its deviations from the mean: $$ \Sigma_x = E[(x - \mu)(x - \mu)^\top] $$ If a linear transformation is applied to $x$ such that $y = Cx$, the covariance of the new variable $y$ becomes: $$ \Sigma_y = E[(Cx - C\mu)(Cx - C\mu)^\top] $$ By using the property of transposes $(AB)^\top = B^\top A^\top$, $C$ matrices can be factored out: $$\begin{align*} \Sigma_y &= E[C(x - \mu)(x - \mu)^\top C^\top] \\ &= C E[(x - \mu)(x - \mu)^\top] C^\top \\ &= C \Sigma_x C^\top \end{align*}$$ The sandwich structure ($C \dots C^T$) ensures that the covariance is always $\geq 0$, symmetric and positive semi-definite, which physically means the output cannot have negative uncertainty.
