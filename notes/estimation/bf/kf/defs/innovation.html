Innovation

Definition

It is the error in the prediction - difference between the actual and the predicted measurement (computed based on the predicted state). Innovation is the error signal that is linearly fedback into an estimator. $$\begin{align*} z_k &= y_k - h_k \\ &= y_k - C\hat{x}_{k|k-1} \\ &= Cx_k + v_k - C\hat{x}_{k|k-1} \end{align*}$$ $h_k = h(\hat{x}_{k|k-1})$ is the measurement model that takes a state as input and returns the measurement expected to be seen from a perfect sensor for that state.
$$\begin{array}{ccl} y &:& \text{Actual measurement} \\ C &:& \text{Measurement/Output matrix} \\ v &:& \text{Measurement noise} \\ y_k = Cx_k + v_k &:& \text{Measurement equation} \end{array}$$
A Gaussian stays Gaussian under linear affine transformations (e.g. linear dynamics or linear measurement model) which is what makes the KF derivation work.

$A,B,C$ are assumed to be LTI. But, the same applies for the time-varying case.

Innovation Covariance

$$\begin{aligned} S_k &= E[z_k z_k^\top] \\ &= E[(Cx_k + v_k - C\hat{x}_{k|k-1})(\cdots)^\top] \\ &= C \underbrace{E \left[ (x_k - \hat{x}_{k|k-1})(\dots)^T \right]}_{\Sigma_{k|k-1}} C^T + \underbrace{E \left[ v_k v_k^T \right]}_{V} \\ &= C \Sigma_{k|k-1} C^T + V \end{aligned}$$ The state $x_k$ and the sensor noise $v_k$ are assumed to be uncorrelated. They can be correlated; for example, IMU readings vary with extreme temperature and can affect state estimation.

Jacobian of the measurement model

For a linear measurement model, the Jacobian is just $C$. $$ H_k = h'(\hat{x}_{k|k-1}) = C_k $$
