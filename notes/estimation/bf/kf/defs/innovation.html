Innovation

Definition

It is the error in the prediction - difference between the actual and the predicted measurement. Innovation is the error signal that is linearly fedback into an estimator. $$\begin{align*} z_k &= y_k - h_k \\ &= y_k - C\hat{x}_{k|k-1} \\ &= Cx_k + v_k - C\hat{x}_{k|k-1} \end{align*}$$
$$\begin{array}{ccl} y &:& \text{Actual measurement} \\ h_k &:& \text{Measurement model} \\ C &:& \text{Measurement/Output matrix} \\ v &:& \text{Measurement noise} \\ y_k = Cx_k + v_k &:& \text{Measurement equation} \end{array}$$
A Gaussian stays Gaussian under linear affine transformations (e.g. linear dynamics or linear measurement model) which is what makes the KF derivation work.

$A,B,C$ are assumed to be LTI. But, the same applies for the time-varying case.

Innovation Covariance

[Expected value] $$\begin{aligned} S_k &= E[z_k z_k^\top] \\ &= E[(Cx_k + v_k - C\hat{x}_{k|k-1})(\cdots)^\top] \\ &= C \underbrace{E \left[ (x_k - \hat{x}_{k|k-1})(\dots)^T \right]}_{\Sigma_{k|k-1}} C^T + \underbrace{E \left[ v_k v_k^T \right]}_{V} \\ &= C_k \Sigma_{k|k-1} C_k^T + V \\ &= H_k \Sigma_{k|k-1} H_k^T + V \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{array}{ccl} V \text{or } R &:& \text{Measurement noise covariance matrix} \\ && \text{Read off a datasheet or determined experimentally. Usually remains constant.} \\ && \text{Captures uncertainty due to sensor inaccuracy.} \end{array}$$
The state $x_k$ and the sensor noise $v_k$ are assumed to be uncorrelated. They can be correlated; for example, IMU readings vary with extreme temperature and can affect state estimation.
