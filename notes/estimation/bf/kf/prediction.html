Prediction step

Overview

The dynamics model is used to propagate the distribution forward by one time step. No measurements are included yet.

State

First, the state estimate or the mean of the distribution is predicted using the dynamics. $$\begin{aligned} \hat{x}_{k|k-1} &= E\left[x_k | y_{1:k-1}\right] \\ &= E\left[ A \hat{x}_{k-1|k-1} + B u_k + w_{k | k-1} \right] \\ &= A \hat{x}_{k-1|k-1} + B u_k \\ &= g(\hat{x}_{k-1|k-1}, u_k, \Delta{t}) \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{array}{ccl} \hat{x}_{k|k-1} &:& \text{State estimate at $k+1$ given measurements up to time $k$.} \\ w_k &:& \text{Stochastic dynamics.} \\ u_k &:& \text{The control input to the system in the previous time step.} \\ g &:& \text{Advances the state $x_k$ based on the system dynamics and the last control input}. \end{array}$$
$u_k$ is known and is not a random variable. A zero mean Gausian noise is assumed, hence $E[w] = 0$. This results in the final equation where the dynamics is applied to the last best state estimate.

Jacobian of the State transition function

$$ G = g'(\hat{x}_{k-1|k-1}, u_k, \Delta{t}) = A_k $$ It is the partial derivative of the state transition function with respect to the state $x$, which is just the $A$ matrix in the case of a Kalman filter.

Even though $g'$ is written as a function of $u$ and $x$, for the linear case, it ignores these inputs and just returns the $A$ matrix. This will not be the case when things become non-linear in EKF. Finding the Jacobian is an overkill for KF, but it makes the math identical to EKF.

Covariance

$$\begin{aligned} \Sigma_{k|k-1} &= E\left[ (x_k - \hat{x}_{k|k-1}) (\cdots)^T \right] \\ &= E\left[ (A x_k + \cancel{B u_k} + w_k - A \hat{x}_{k-1|k-1} - \cancel{B u_k}) (\cdots)^T \right] \\ &= A \underbrace{E\left[ (x_k - \hat{x}_{k-1|k-1}) (\cdots)^T \right]}_{\Sigma_{k-1|k-1}} A^T + \underbrace{E\left[ w_k w_k^T \right]}_{W} \\ &= A \Sigma_{k-1|k-1} A^T + W \\ &= G \Sigma_{k-1|k-1} G^T + W \\ \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{array}{ccl} W &:& \text{Process covariance} \end{array}$$
$A$ and $E[.]$ are linear, so $A$ can be taken outside. $x_k$ and $w_k$ are uncorrelated; hence the product is 0.

Intuition

The end equation passes the state covariance through the dynamics and adds on the process noise to make the noise bigger.

$A \Sigma_{k-1|k-1} A^T$ is how a coariance matrix is propagated through a linear transformation [ref]. A change of coordinates takes place on the matrix. It shows up all the time where a linear change of coordinates is applied to a matrix that is in a different coordinate system.
