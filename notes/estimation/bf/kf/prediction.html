Prediction step

Overview

The dynamics model is used to propagate the distribution forward by one time step. No measurements are included yet.

State

First, the state estimate or the mean of the distribution is predicted using the dynamics. $$\begin{aligned} \hat{x}_{k|k-1} &= E\left[x_k | y_{1:k-1}\right] \\ &= E\left[ A \hat{x}_{k-1|k-1} + B u_{k-1} + w_{k | k-1} \right] \\ &= A \hat{x}_{k-1|k-1} + B u_{k-1} \\ &= g(\hat{x}_{k-1|k-1}, u_{k-1}, \Delta{t}) \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{array}{ccl} \hat{x}_{k|k-1} &:& \text{State estimate at $k$ given measurements up to time $k-1$.} \\ w_k &:& \text{Stochastic dynamics.} \\ u_{k-1} &:& \text{The control input to the system in the previous time step.} \\ g &:& \text{Advances to state $x_k$ based on the system dynamics and the last control input}. \end{array}$$
$u_{k-1}$ is known and is not a random variable. A zero mean Gausian noise is assumed, hence $E[w] = 0$ [Refer process noise]. This results in the final equation where the dynamics is applied to the last best state estimate.

Jacobian of the State transition function

It aims to answer the question, how sensitive is the current-state prediction $\hat{x}_{k|k-1}$, to errors in the previous state estimate $\hat{x}_{k-1|k-1}$. $$\begin{align*} G &= \frac{\partial g}{\partial{\hat{x}_{k-1|k-1}}} \\ &= g'(\hat{x}_{k-1|k-1}, u_{k-1}, \Delta{t}) \\ & = A_{k-1} \end{align*}$$ Even though $g'$ is written as a function of $u$ and $x$, for the linear case, it ignores these inputs and just returns the $A$ matrix. This will not be the case when things become non-linear in EKF. Finding the Jacobian is an overkill for KF, but it makes the math identical to EKF.

Covariance

$$\begin{aligned} \Sigma_{k|k-1} &= E\left[ (x_k - \hat{x}_{k|k-1}) (\cdots)^T \right] \\ &= E\left[ (A x_{k-1} + \cancel{B u_{k-1}} + w_k - A \hat{x}_{k-1|k-1} - \cancel{B u_{k-1}}) (\cdots)^T \right] \\ &= A \underbrace{E\left[ (x_{k-1} - \hat{x}_{k-1|k-1}) (\cdots)^T \right]}_{\Sigma_{k-1|k-1}} A^T + \underbrace{E\left[ w_k w_k^T \right]}_{W} \\ &= A \Sigma_{k-1|k-1} A^T + W \\ &= G \Sigma_{k-1|k-1} G^T + W \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{array}{ccl} W &:& \text{Process covariance} \\ x_k &:& \text{Modeled state without considering any measurements} \end{array}$$
$A$ and $E[.]$ are linear, so $A$ can be taken outside. $x_k$ and $w_k$ are uncorrelated; hence the product is 0 [Process noise].

The modeled system has inherent unpredictability captured by $w_k$ - unmodeled dynamics, disturbances, wind gusts, imperfect actuators, discretization error, whatever the model doesn't capture. On the other hand, the prediction has no noise term, because it is not possible to predict noise — it's zero-mean and unknown by definition.

Intuition

The end equation passes the state covariance through the dynamics and adds on the process noise to make the noise bigger.

$A \Sigma_{k-1|k-1} A^T$ is how a coariance matrix is propagated through a linear transformation [ref]. A change of coordinates takes place on the matrix. It shows up all the time where a linear change of coordinates is applied to a matrix that is in a different coordinate system.
