Measurement update

State update

$$ \hat{x}_{k|k} = \hat{x}_{k|k-1} + L_k \, z_k $$
$$\begin{array}{ccl} z_k &:& \text{Innovation} \end{array}$$
$L_k$ is the Kalman gain

Covariance update

$$ \Sigma_{k|k} = E[(x_k - \hat{x}_{k|k})(\dots)^\top] $$ Substituting the state update equation and $z_k$: $$ \Sigma_{k|k} = E[(x_k - \hat{x}_{k|k-1} - L_k(Cx_k + v_k - C\hat{x}_{k|k-1}))(\dots)^\top] $$ Assuming the state $x_k$ and the sensor noise $v_k$ are uncorrelated: $$ \Sigma_{k|k} = (I - L_kC) \Sigma_{k|k-1} (I - L_kC)^\top + L_k V L_k^\top $$
$$\begin{array}{ccl} V = E\left[v_k v_k^T \right] &:& \text{Measurement noise} \end{array}$$
This form of covariance update is called the Joseph form. The updated covariance $\Sigma_{k|k}$ is a function of $L$ and is a convex quadratic equation.

Numerical precision

The Joseph form is good in terms of numerical precision w.r.t. floating point numbers on a computer because it's symmetric. This form is guaranteed to give a symmetric positive semidefinite matrix by construction.

Covariances have to be at least positive semidefinite.

Other versions give non-symmetric matrices or have minus signs which can lead to indefinite covariances i.e. covariance with negative eigen values.
