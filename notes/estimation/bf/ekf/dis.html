Disadvantages

If a system is highly nonlinear or the uncertainty is wide, the edges of the distribution will hit the actual curves that EKF ignores.

EKF forces a perfect, symmetrical Gaussian ellipse over the data by linearising a nonlinear system. It does this by approximating a nonlinear curved system using a straight tangent line at the mean (current state estimate).

Assume the true distribution is a banana shaped curve. Because EKF ignores the curvature (the higher-order Taylor terms) of the system, it places the center of the ellipse/distribution in the empty space inside the curve of the banana. The filter now confidently believes the system is in a state where it physically cannot be, i.e. the new mean can be wildly off-center, and the new covariance can be dangerously overconfident.
