Continuous time

Consider there is a small error in the acceleration due to more force being applied based on a model error. Assume the initial velocity and position is 0 for simplicity. Focusing on the error alone. Integrating acceleration and then the velocity gives: $$\begin{align*} \dot{q}(t) &= \int^t_0{\epsilon dT} = \epsilon t + \dot{q}(0) = \epsilon t \\ q(t) &= \int^t_0{\epsilon t dT}\\ q(t) - q(0) &= \frac{1}{2} \epsilon t^2 \end{align*}$$ The smallest deviation of a model (used by the controller to make its control decisions) from the real world can lead to position errors that grow quadratically with time in a double integrator system.

A double integrator system is an unstable system. A stable system would have some self-correcting ability and the mismatch would not grow infinitely.
