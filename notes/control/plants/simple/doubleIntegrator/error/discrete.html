Discrete time

Setup

Consider a double integrator where acceleration $u$ is being controlled. The continuous system is: $$ \ddot{x}(t) = u(t) + w(t) $$ $w(t)$ is a constant model error. For example, an unmodeled constant bias in acceleration, like gravity or calibration offset.

Discretising

Discretising the system with a control period $\Delta t$, the state-update equations for velocity $v_k$ and position $x_k$ at step $k$ are: $$\begin{align*} v_{k+1} &= v_k + (u_k + w)\Delta t \\ x_{k+1} &= x_k + v_k\Delta t + \frac{1}{2}(u_k + w)(\Delta t)^2 \end{align*}$$

Error accumulation

Assuming zero initial error. Focusing only on the cumulative effect of the constant model error $w$ over $n$ steps.

At each step, a velocity error of $w \Delta t$ is added. After $i$ steps, the velocity error is the linear sum: $$e_{v, i} = i \cdot w \Delta t$$

Position error at step $n$ is the sum of all past velocity errors, plus the direct acceleration error from the current step. Substituting the above velocity error equation: $$\begin{align*} e_{x, n} &= \sum_{i=1}^{n} e_{v, i-1} \Delta t + n \cdot \frac{1}{2} w (\Delta t)^2 \\ &= w(\Delta t)^2 \left[ \sum_{i=1}^{n} (i-1) + \frac{n}{2} \right] \\ &= \frac{1}{2} w \cdot n^2 (\Delta t)^2 \end{align*}$$ $\sum_{i=1}^{n-1} i = \frac{n(n-1)}{2}$

If the total elapsed time is $T = n \Delta t$, the accumulated position error becomes: $$ e_{x, n} = \frac{1}{2} w T^2 $$

Because of the $n^2$ dependency, the error at a specific physical time $T$ remains $\frac{1}{2} w T^2$, regardless of how fine the control period is. In an open-loop system, that quadratic growth will quickly run away. This is why double integrators require active feedback control to continuously drain this compounding error before it blows up.
