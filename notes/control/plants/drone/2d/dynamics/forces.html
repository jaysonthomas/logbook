Forces

Decomposition

The thrust vector $F_t$ can be decomposed into vertical and horizontal components in the directions of the coordinate axis of the world frame. Since they meet at a right angle, trigonometry can be used: $$\begin{align*} F_y &= F_t \cdot sin\phi \\ F_z &= F_t \cdot cos\phi \\ \end{align*}$$ The thrust produced by each of the 2 rotors is given as: $$\begin{align*} F_1 &= k_f \cdot \omega_1^2 \\ F_2 &= k_f \cdot \omega_2^2 \\ \end{align*}$$

Translational acceleration

Vertical: $$\begin{align*} \ddot{z} &= g - \frac{F_t \cdot cos\phi}{m} \\ &= g - \frac{(F_1 + F_2) \cdot cos\phi}{m} \end{align*}$$ Lateral: $$ \ddot{y} = \frac{(F_1 + F_2) \cdot sin\phi}{m} $$

Note

Forces are vectors. When two forces point in the exact same direction, they add together. If each rotor pushes air downward with 10N of force, the drone experiences a total upward reaction force of 20N. If the total thrust were the average, two rotors producing 10N each would only generate 10N of lift. This would mean adding a second motor gives zero extra lifting power, which isn't the case.
