Moments

Roll

Consider a 2D drone with its axis of rotation (into the screen, $X$) at its center.

A force acting perpendicular to the axis of rotation at some distance from it, produces a moment about the rotation axis causing the vehicle to rotate.

Here, each rotor applies a vertical force along $Z_W$ producing a moment about $X_W$.

Magnitude of rolling torque

When the body frame is aligned with the drone

$$ M_x = F \cdot L $$ $F$, only for the context of calculating $M_x$ is given as: $$ F = F_1 - F_2 $$ The convention might be to deliberately subtract $F_2$, because if $F_2 > F_1$, then the drone rolls in the anti-clockwise direction which means a negative torque ($M_x < 0$) should have caused this; the combination of $F_1, F_2$ gives the negative torque value.

When the body frame is not aligned with the drone

The drone's arms would be rotated by $\theta$ w.r.t. the body-frame coordinate system $B$.
Since a drone's body frame rotates with the drone, from the side view, the force $F$ acts at a shorter perpendicular distance $d$ from the rotation axis $X_B$. $$ M_x = F \cdot Lcos\theta $$ $\theta$ represents the angle between $Y_B$ and the drone's arm in the positive $XY_B$ plane. It is not the yaw angle $\psi$ caused by a reaction torque.

Angular/rotational acceleration

$$\begin{align*} \ddot{\phi} &= \frac{M_x}{I_x} \\ &= \frac{(F_1 - F_2)\cdot Lcos\theta}{I_x} \\ &= \frac{u_2 \cdot Lcos\theta}{I_x} \end{align*}$$ The rotational moment, $M_x = u_2 = F_1 - F_2$

Notes

The coordinate system that is used most often for a quadrotor body frame has the x and y axes of the body frame pointing half-way between the rotor arms, i.e. at $45^0$. This is the reason why $\sqrt{2}$ is often seen in quadrotor math, since $cos45 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$.
