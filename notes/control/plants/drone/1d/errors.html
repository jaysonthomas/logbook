Errors

Setup

Consider that the aim is for a monorotor to hover: $$\ddot{z} = 0$$ The target position is a constant altitude (since the aim is to hover).

Initial state: $z(0) = \dot{z}(0) = 0$

Effect of a small model error

Assume the drone's mass has been overestimated by a small amount, i.e. the drone is lighter than the controller thinks. This means that the controller would apply more torque to the rotors and hence more thrust to the drone than is necessary.

Consider the drone in the hovering state. Due to the mass mismatch, there would be a small constant error $\epsilon$ in the drone's linear acceleration. The position over time can be found as follows:

  • Integrating $\ddot{z}$ to get velocity
    • $$ \dot{z}(t) = \int^t_0{\epsilon dT} = \epsilon t + \dot{z}(0) = \epsilon t $$
  • Integrating $\dot{z}$ to get position
    • $$\begin{align*} z(t) &= \int^t_0{\epsilon t dT}\\ z(t) - z(0) &= \frac{1}{2} \epsilon t^2 \end{align*}$$
With a small error in mass, the position error grows quadratically with time.
The smallest deviation of a model (used by the controller to make its thrust decisions) from the real world can lead to quadratic errors in double integrator systems as seen above. A double integrator system is an unstable system. A stable system would have some self-correcting ability and the mismatch would not grow infinitely.

Error sources

  • Model error
    • An error in the model of a vehicle. Eg: neglected aerodynamic effects, model parameter uncertainties like rotational inertia.

  • Disturbances
    • Errors caused by something external or internal to a vehicle that wasn't anticipated. For example, a strong cross-breeze or a part coming loose.

  • Measurement error
