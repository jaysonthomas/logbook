Forces

Decomposition

Thrust

When the rotor spins, it pushes air downwards. By Newton's third law, the air pushes the rotor (and the vehicle) upwards with an equal and opposite force called thrust. The force that actually acts on the drone points upwards.

Gravity

A normal force refers to a contact force exerted by a solid surface perpendicular to the object resting on it. Because a monorotor is airborne and hovering freely, there is no solid surface beneath it, so there is no normal force acting on it. Gravity acts downwards.

Drag

A quadrotor as a whole and the individual propellers experience drag.

In practice, drag is treated as a disturbance when designing quadrotor controllers. It isn't modeled explicitly since:

  • Its effects are relatively small at lower vehicle and wind speeds
  • It is difficult to identify the correct drag model

Net upward force

A monorotor has only one rotor and therefore only one thrust vector pointing upwards in the positive $z$ direction. This will cause an acceleration in the z direction. $$ F_z = m|g| - |F_t| $$ $F_z$ is the net force on the vehicle. To hover ($F_z=0$), the thrust force upwards would need to be equal to the force that gravity exerts downwards. z is positive downwards.

Linear translational acceleration

$$ \ddot{z} = g - \frac{F_t}{m} $$

Translational equilibrium

When a monorotor is hovering, it is in translational equilibrium.
