Overview

Computation for the discrete-time LQR solved via dynamic programming (the Riccati recursion) starts with a backward pass, from the final time step $N$ and works backwards to time step 0.At each step $k$, this recursion spits out two matrices (or vectors, depending on the state dimension): $P_k$ and $K_k$.

Intuition

The backward pass can be thought of as a scouting mission traveling backward from the finish line to the starting line:
  • At step $k+1$
    • The scout looks ahead at the remaining track and says, "If we land here, the optimal cost to finish the race from this point onward is characterized by $P_{k+1}$."

  • At step $k$
    • The scout looks at the physics of a system ($A$ and $B$), the immediate penalties ($Q$ and $R$), and the future danger ($P_{k+1}$) and writes down a recipe: "If you find yourself at state $x_k$, apply action $u_k = -K_k x_k$. Doing this will minimize your immediate effort and land you in the safest possible position for the next step, yielding a total expected remaining cost of $x_k^T P_k x_k$."

  • The Update
    • The scout saves $P_k$ as the new benchmark, steps backward to $k-1$, and repeats the process. By the time the computation reaches $k=0$, the backward pass would have mapped out the entire terrain ($P$) and created a playbook ($K$) for exactly how aggressive or gentle the control inputs need to be at every single moment in time.
