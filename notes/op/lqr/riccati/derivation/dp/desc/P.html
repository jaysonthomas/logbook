Cost-to-go

Overview

$P_k$ represents the minimum remaining cost that will be accumulated from time step $k$ until the very end of the horizon, assuming the controller acts optimally.

It is not possible to know how expensive it is to be at step $k$ until the cost of handling the aftermath at step $k+1$ is known. Hence why $P$ must be calculated backward from the terminal penalty $P_N = Q_f$.

If a state has a high cost-to-go, the controller has to work hard to get out of there.

Strictly speaking:

  • $V(x)$ is the cost-to-go (a scalar value).
  • $P$ is the cost-to-go matrix (or the Riccati matrix).
Control engineers often use lazy shorthand and say "$P$ is the cost-to-go," but what they actually mean is that $P$ is the matrix that parameterizes the quadratic cost-to-go function.
