Introduction

Overview

LQR problem is a trajectory optimisation problem AKA a deterministic optimal control problem.

Here cost functions as opposed to gains, are tuned.

The initial state is always given.

The only constraints allowed for an LQR problem are those for the dynamics. Linear dynamics is assumed.

Advantages

  • The reason LQR is so useful is because most nonlinear problems can be locally approximated as an LQR one. This is useful in creating local tracking controllers around trajectories and stabilizing controllers around equilibrium points.

  • Computationally tractable even with problems with states of many dimensions. Able to solve them quickly.

  • Can be applied to a variety of problems like stochastic, partially observable, infinite horizon problems etc.

  • LQR is a rare gem where there's a closed form solution. It is possible to get a lot of insight and leverage from such a closed form solution in different areas. When constraints other than those of the dynamics are added in, it ends up not having a closed form solution. It would then need to be solved numerically, which is also done very often.
