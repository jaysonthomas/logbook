Thread

Overview

Since the administration of processes is computationally taxing (switching between and scheduling processes), OSs support a more resource-friendly way of realizing concurrent operations through threads.

A thread represents a concurrent execution unit within a process. They are characterised as light-weight processes.

Differences with a process

Creation & destruction

Threads are significantly easier to create and destroy. In many systems the creation of a thread is up to 100 times faster than the creation of a process. This is especially advantageous in situations, when the need for concurrent operations changes dynamically.

Resources

Each process has its own address space. Threads exist within processes and share their resources. A thread does not require a new address space to be created.

All the threads in a process can access its shared memory. Threads also share other OS dependent resources such as processors, files, and network connections. As a result, the management overhead for threads is typically less than for processes.

Threads, however, are not protected against each other and must carefully synchronize when accessing the shared process resources to avoid conflicts.
