Error twist

Overview

$$ [V_{err}]=log(X^{-1}X_{d}) $$
$$\begin{array}{ccl} X = T_{se} &:& \text{current configuration of {e} w.r.t. {s}.} \\ X_{d} = T_{sd} &:& \text{desired/reference configuration of {e} w.r.t. {s}.} \end{array}$$
$$ X^{-1}X_d = (T_{se})^{-1} T_{sd} = T_{es} T_{sd} = T_{ed} $$ $T_{ed}$ is the homogeneous transformation matrix that represents the desired frame {d} relative to the current end-effector frame {e}.
  • If the EE is perfectly on track, {e} and {d} are identical, meaning $T_{ed} = I$.
  • If there is a tracking error, $T_{ed}$ tells how much the EE needs to translate and rotate from the current position to reach the target.

Lie Algebra: The Logarithm

Because $SE(3)$ (the space of rigid-body transformations) is a curved manifold, it is not possible to simply subtract two transformation matrices ($X_d - X$) to find the error. It wouldn't yield a valid transformation matrix, nor a meaningful velocity vector.

The matrix logarithm maps an element from the Lie Group $SE(3)$ to its Lie Algebra $se(3)$. In robotics, this is called the matrix representation of a twist, denoted by $[\mathcal{V}]$.

$$ [\mathcal{V}_{err}] = \log(T_{ed}) = \begin{bmatrix} [\omega_{err}] & v_{err} \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} $$
