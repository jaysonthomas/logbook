Feedforward reference twist

The aim is to calculate the velocity required to move from the current desired to the next desired trajectory pose in the span of one control loop cycle ($\Delta t$). $$\begin{align*} ^{G_{d_k}}X^{G_{d_{k+1}}} &= X^{{G_{d_k}}^{-1}}X^{G_{d_{k+1}}} \\ [^{G_{d_k}}V^{G_{d_{k+1}}}] &= \frac{1}{\Delta{t}}log(^{G_{d_k}}X^{G_{d_{k+1}}}) \\ [^{G_{d_k}}V^{G_{d_{k+1}}}] &\rightarrow ^{G_{d_k}\hspace{-0.6em}}V^{G_{d_{k+1}}} \end{align*}$$ $\rightarrow$ denotes conversion from se(3) to a $6 \times 1$ vector.

Breakdown of steps

The matrix logarithm calculates the rigid-body displacement required to bridge the gap between $X^{G_{d_k}}$ and $X^{G_{d_{k+1}}}$. The displacement can also be viewed as an instantaneous change/velocity to go between the 2 configurations. The change is so small that it can be considered to occur over an infinitely small period of time. This is called an infinitesimal motion.

In order to map the instantaneous motion to a motion that occurs over a finite period of time $\Delta{t}$, the output of the logarithm is divided/scaled by $\Delta{t}$. The resulting velocity is kept constant for the time interval.
