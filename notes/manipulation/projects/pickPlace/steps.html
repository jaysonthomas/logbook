Steps

Trajectory generation

Gripper poses are designed as a function of time using a trajectory generator. Interpolate in between. Bring in the rest of the arm later.

Plot frames for the open loop trajectory of poses the hand needs to go through in space at different timestamps.

Joint space mapping

The aim is to obtain corresponding joint positions/commands as a function of time which are fed as input to the controller. $$ X^G(t) \rightarrow q(t) $$ Decide a $q(0)$. Then integrate forward using instantaneous joint velocities $\dot{q}$ to get $q(t)$. Joint velocities are obtained by taking the inverse jacobian of the spatial velocity: $$ \dot{q}(t) = [J^G(q)]^{-1}V^G(t) $$
$$\begin{array}{ccl} V^G(t) &:& \text{Spatial velocity of the gripper from the reference trajectory.} \end{array}$$
To avoid singularities, the Jacobian needs to be invertible. Else, it needs to be addressed with more sophisticated methods. Intuitively, to get the smoothness in joint position transitions from one time instant to the next, the derivative of the kinematics (jacobian is approximately the derivative of FK) is used to design controllers.

Overall steps

Generate a reference trajectory. The program then loops through the $N$ reference configurations, $N-1$ times. For the $i^{th}$ iteration, the controller uses $X_{d,i}$ and $X_{d,i+1}$ to calculate the feedforward twist $V_{d}$.

Each time through the loop:

  • Using feedback control, find the wheel and joint speeds (controls)
  • Find the next configuration by passing the controls, current configuration and the timestep to the simulation.
  • Store every $k^{th}$ configuration for later animation.
  • Store every $k^{th}$ $X_{err}$ to plot the evolution of the error over time.

After the loop, write the $\frac{N}{k}$ configurations to a csv file.

The initial and final configurations of the cube is given. Initial reference configuration of the ee is also given. Choose an initial configuration of the youBot so that the end-effector has at least 30 degrees of orientation error and 0.2 m of position error. The controller will need to be designed in such a way so as to drive all initial error to zero by the end of the first trajectory segment; otherwise, your grasp operation may fail.
