End effector twist

Overview

An end-effector twist represents the velocity of the end-effector itself.

A twist, also known as a spatial velocity, combines both the linear velocity and the angular velocity of a rigid body (in this case, the end-effector) into a single 6-dimensional vector.

Math

$$ \mathcal{V} = \begin{bmatrix} \omega \\ v \end{bmatrix} $$
$$\begin{array}{ccl} \omega \in \mathbb{R}^3 &:& \text{The angular velocity vector (how fast and around what axes the end-effector is rotating).} \\ v \in \mathbb{R}^3 &:& \text{The linear velocity vector (how fast the end-effector's origin is moving).} \end{array}$$

Relation to joints

Ee twist is directly caused by the velocities of the joints. They are mapped together using the Geometric Jacobian matrix ($J$): $$\mathcal{V} = J(q)\dot{q}$$
$$\begin{array}{ccl} \dot{q} &:& \text{Joint velocity (how fast the joints are moving).} \\ J(q) &:& \text{Jacobian, which changes depending on the current configuration of the joints.} \\ \end{array}$$
