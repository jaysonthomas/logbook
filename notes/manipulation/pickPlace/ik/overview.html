Overview

Definition

Inverse kinematics is a nonlinear complicated function that maps a gripper pose in the world to joint positions. $$ q = (f^G_{kin})^{-1} \, ^WX^G $$

Discontinuity

IK is harder than FK since the mapping in IK is not always unique:
  • There can be multiple joint angle solutions for the same ee pose in the world which can lead to discontinuity.
  • There can also be no solutions.
Both cases would involve arm movements that jump around to different solutions in very short time spans, for e.g. every 0.0001 seconds which wouldn't be good. IK is normally used with additional requirements to maintain continuity/smoothness/centering etc.

All the complexities are due to 3D rotations.

Solutions

Even though $f^G_{kin}$ consists of a simple series of transforms, it has a lot of nonlinear/non-trivial equations in $q$ which makes it hard to invert and analyse. On top of that, the mapping function may also be rectangular and hence non-invertible.

Diff IK can be used instead which solves the IK problem by considering the differential kinematics (first order). The mapping matrix, the Jacobian, is linear which simplifies things. However, a Jacobian is not always a square matrix. Hence, taking the inverse is still a problem.

In order to use rectangular matrices, pseudo-inverse is used along with diff IK. It tries to solve an optimisation problem wherein the aim is to find the minimum joint velocities that can achieve the desired spatial velocity, as close as possible.
