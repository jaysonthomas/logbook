Pseudo Inverse

Overview

Given $\dot{q} = [J^B(q)]^{-1} V^B$, to find $\dot{q}$, the pseudo-inverse of $J$ is computed since $J$ is not always invertible, i.e. it may be rectangular, having an unequal number of rows and columns. $$ \dot{q} = [J^B(q)]^{\dagger}V^B $$

An optimisation problem

[QP]
When a matrix is not square, the inverse could also have infinitely many or no solutions. pinv however takes the best-effort action and is well-defined even for a non-square matrix. A Pseudo-Inverse controller is magical because it solves an optimisation problem.

The problem is to find $\dot{q}$, such that the LHS is approximately equal (as close as possible) to the RHS. $$ J^G(q)\dot{q} \approx V^G $$

When $q$ is measured, then $J^G(q)$ becomes a simple matrix and the equation becomes linear, of the form: $$ A x \approx b $$ The optimisation aims to minimise the least squares error: $$ \| J^G(q)\dot{q} - V^G\|^2 $$

Different conditions

Assuming $V$ is $m$-dimensional and $q$ is $n$-dimensional, $J^\dagger$ is $n\times m$.

$n=m$

$J$ is a square matrix. The psuedo-inverse of $J$ is equivalent to its inverse. It means that if there is any solution for the joint space, it is a unique solution.

$n < m$

The rank of $J$ is $n$ - the joint variable vector lives in a lower dimension space than the operational space vector, i.e. $J$ is not full-rank. In this case, there can be configurations in the operational space for which there is no solution in the joint space, i.e. the inverse becomes singular or non-invertible. The determinant is zero.

In this case where there might be no solutions, pinv does its best effort to find a $\dot{q}$ that achieves the nearest possible match to the desired spatial velocity.

$n > m$

The joint space dimension is higher than that of the operational space. For a configuration in the operation space, there can be more than one solution in the joint space. A null-space term, which maps any joint space vector to a zero vector in the operational space is used to accommodate the extra solutions.

In this case where there might be many solutions, pinv picks the one that's minimum norm in the least squares norm in the null space. It finds the smallest solution. In this case, it would be the smallest joint velocities that achieve the desired spatial velocity.
