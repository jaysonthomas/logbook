Relation

Exponential and logarithmic maps help move between the groups (poses) and the algebra (velocities):
  • $\exp([\mathcal{V}]) \to T$: Integrating a velocity twist over a unit time gives the pose change in $SE(3)$.
    • $$ se(3) \xrightarrow[]{\exp} SE(3) $$
  • $\log(T) \to [\mathcal{V}]$: Matrix log of a relative pose gives the error twist in $\mathfrak{se}(3)$ required to get there.
    • $$ SE(3) \xrightarrow[]{\log} se(3) $$

Intuition

Considering the globe and flat paper analogy.

Lie algebra is useful because it acts as a local map of the lie group. If one chooses a velocity vector on that flat sheet of paper and starts walking in that direction at a steady speed, one is charting a path. If that flat sheet is wrapped around the globe, one is performing the exponential map ($\exp$). It takes a straight velocity vector from the flat tangent space and rolls it onto a curved, valid path on the group's surface.

Conversely, the logarithm projects a point on the curved globe onto the flat sheet of paper to see what vector velocity would have driven one there from the North Pole.
