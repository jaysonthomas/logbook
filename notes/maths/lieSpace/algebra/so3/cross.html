Simplification of cross products

In physics and robotics, the cross product $\omega \times p$ is everywhere. It calculates the linear velocity of a point $p$ spinning around an axis $\omega$.

However, the cross product is algebraically annoying because it doesn't follow standard matrix-algebra rules. It is not possible to cleanly isolate variables or compute derivatives easily when things are bound inside a $\times$ operator.

The standard determinant definition of the cross product $\omega \times p$: $$ \omega \times p = \begin{bmatrix} \omega_y p_z - \omega_z p_y \\ \omega_z p_x - \omega_x p_z \\ \omega_x p_y - \omega_y p_x \end{bmatrix} $$ Factoring out the coordinates of $p$ and writing this purely as a linear system of equations (a matrix multiplied by $p$): $$\begin{bmatrix} \text{Result}_x \\ \text{Result}_y \\ \text{Result}_z \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \cdot p_x - \omega_z \cdot p_y + \omega_y \cdot p_z \\ \omega_z \cdot p_x + 0 \cdot p_y - \omega_x \cdot p_z \\ -\omega_y \cdot p_x + \omega_x \cdot p_y + 0 \cdot p_z \end{bmatrix}$$ Pulling the coefficients of $p$ out into their own matrix, gives the skew-symmetric matrix $[\omega]$: $$\omega \times p = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & -\omega_z & \omega_y \\ \omega_z & 0 & -\omega_x \\ -\omega_y & \omega_x & 0 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} p_x \\ p_y \\ p_z \end{bmatrix} = [\omega]p $$ The bracket notation $[\omega]$ is a mathematical trick that transforms a cross product into a standard matrix multiplication. It allows the angular velocity vector to be treated as a linear operator. This is incredibly powerful because it allows the use of standard linear algebra tools (like matrix exponentials, inverses, and derivatives) on rotations.
