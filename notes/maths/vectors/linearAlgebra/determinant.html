Determinant

Overview

[Ref]

The determinant gives an idea of how much an area (in 2D and volume in 3D) is being squished, scaled or flipped. If a determinant of a matrix is greater than 1, it is going to stretch vectors. If it is less than 1, it is going to shrink vectors.

Consider a world frame. Its basis vectors can be written as a $3 \times 3$ matrix. The determinant gives the volume enclosed by the convex hull formed by the basis/column vectors. Here, the convex hull is a box.

One caveat is that it gives the signed volume.

Signed volume

Consider a unit orthogonal frame that obeys the right hand rule. If the 3rd vector (z) or 3rd column of a matrix where the the columns are formed by the basis vectors of the frame, points upwards, then it's positive volume. Else, negative.

If the sign is negative, the transformation has turned space inside out. A right hand has been transformed into a left hand. This only happens if a reflection (a mirror flip) has occurred.

Pure rotation usecase

For a matrix to represent a pure rotation, it must satisfy two conditions:
  • It can't stretch or shrink vectors (distances between points must stay the same).
  • It can't turn vectors inside out (an object in 3D space cannot be rotated and suddenly become its own mirror image).
