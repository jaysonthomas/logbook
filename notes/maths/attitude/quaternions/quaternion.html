Quaternion

Overview

It is based on the generalisation of complex numbers in 4D. They've got algrebraic properties that make them useful. $$\begin{align*} q &= \begin{bmatrix} w & x & y & z \end{bmatrix} \\ 1 &= w^2 + x^2 + y^2 + z^2 \end{align*}$$ $$ q = \begin{bmatrix} cos(\frac{\theta}{2}) \\ a\,sin(\frac{\theta}{2}) \end{bmatrix} $$

Initialisation

Set w=1 and the axis to the axis of interest. Or, use euler angles and convert to quaternion.

Quirks

Flipping the sign of a unit quaternion gives the same angle.
