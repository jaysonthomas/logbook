Overview

Definition

It is the rotation of a body frame to an inertially fixed frame, usually the world frame. It rotates a vector defined in the body frame into the world frame.

It has 3DOFs. But, there is no globally non-singular way of representing it with just 3 numbers. Locally, it is possible for small rotations.

Representation

A Jacobian mapping euler angles into anything else can get singular. E.g. inverse kinematics.

Quaternions and rotation matrices are singularity free - they don't have places where the jacobians blow up. E.g. doing weird things at 90 degrees.

But they have some quirks namely they are not a minimal coordinate representation. Rotations involve 3 DOFs. But quaternions have 4 numbers and rotation matrices have nine numbers. They are overparameterised. This means that, quaternions have 1 constraint and rotation matrices have 6 constraints.

These constraints can be baked into an optimisation problem. But, there are better ways.
