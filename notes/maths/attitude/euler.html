Euler's equation

[Ref]

General form of Euler's equation in a rotating frame: $$ M = I\dot\omega + \omega \times (I\omega) $$

Euler's rotation theorem

It states that any 3D rotation or sequence of rotations can be described as a single rotation by an angle $\theta$ around a single 3D unit vector axis $\mathbf{u} = (x, y, z)$.

Disadvantages of euler angles

  • Numerical Singularities

    • Euler angles suffer from Gimbal Lock and other singularities where the mathematical representation breaks down, causing Jacobians to become singular.

  • Numerical Instability

    • They are prone to poor numerical behavior, often requiring messy, "hacky" workarounds that can lead to failures in control systems.

  • Mathematical Complexity

    • They involve excessive trigonometric functions, which makes them less efficient and harder to work with than alternatives like rotation matrices or quaternions.

Gimbal lock

[Ref: GuerillaCG youtube]

Rotation axes are chained together where rotation about 1 axis can change the orientation of another rotation axis. It is useful to think about euler rotations in terms of the hierarchy of rotation axes.

Gimbal lock occurs when 2 rotation axes align. When a gimbal lock occurs, an object loses a degree of freedom - it will not be possible to rotate the object in a certain direction (either roll pitch or yaw) since 2 of the 3 rotation axes align with each other.

In order to get out of the lock and achieve the desired rotation about the missing DOF/rotation axis, all 3 axes would need to be rotated. This would cause the object to rotate in an unexpected arc (as compared to a straight arc if there was no gimbal lock) in between key frames.
