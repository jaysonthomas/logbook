Sensitivity & resolution

Sensitivity

It configures the Measurement Range or Full Scale Range of the accelerometer, i.e. the range of forces the accelerometer can measure, say between -8g and +8g. If the device experiences a force greater than 8g (like a hard impact), the sensor will clip and not measure the excess.

In physics, sensitivity usually refers to the smallest change a sensor can detect which differs from this definition.

Resolution

Considering an accelerometer. It tells how many raw digital counts (LSBs) correspond to 1 milli-g (1 mg = $0.001 \times 9.81$ m/s²) of actual physical acceleration.

An LSB, least significant bit, is the smallest increment a digital sensor's output can change by, i.e., one step in the raw digital reading coming out of the ADC.

4.096 LSB/mg means that for every 1 milli-g of acceleration, the digital output increases by approximately 4 counts. Example: If a sensor reads a raw value of 4096, that equals 1000 mg or 1g.

For a gyroscope, the unit would be LSB/dps (degrees per second).

Relationship between resolution and sensitivity

Since an ADC has a fixed number of bits, changing the range (sensitivity) forces the resolution to change. They are inversely proportional.

If the range is increased to 8g to measure bigger impacts, the available bits are stretched over a wider area. 1 LSB corresponds to $\frac{16000}{65536} = 0.244$mg assuming a 16-bit ADC.

If the range is decreased to 2g to measure tiny movements, the resolution gets better (more LSBs per mg). 1 LSB corresponds to $\frac{4000}{65536} = 0.061$mg.
